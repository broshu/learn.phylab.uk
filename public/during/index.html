Draw the field lines

A positive and a negative charge of the same size. Draw the electric field lines around them, as many as you need to show the shape of the field. Draw each line in the direction the field points; the arrow follows your finger or mouse. When you check, the system looks only for big mistakes, such as lines that cross.

In the real class, students draw two or three of these, for example also two equal positive charges.

Before class

Peer feedback

Help a classmate improve their drawing. You are not marking it: point out what you notice and ask questions, the way you would want feedback on your own drawing.

3D match

Field lines do not lie in a flat page: they fill the space around the charges. First explore the field in three dimensions, then match a field you are shown.

Explore the space

Place charges and charged objects above the table and turn it to look from every side. Try one charge on its own; a positive and a negative charge; +2q with −q; a charged plate; a positive and a negative plate facing each other (a parallel-plate capacitor); a point charge above a plate; the charged rod and the charged ring.

Match the field

The orange lines show a field, but the objects that make it are hidden. Choose objects from the trays and place them so that they make the same field. Count the lines: the number of lines leaving or reaching a charge is proportional to its charge. Your own field lines stay hidden while you work; check to see them over the orange ones.
