With help

Learn to hunt

You are a young platypus. Under water you shut your eyes, ears and nostrils, and find food with your bill, which feels the weak electric fields that living prey give off. Before your first hunt, your mother shows you what the field of a shrimp looks like. In this part you may ask her for help.

The model: a shrimp is treated as an electric dipole and the bill as reading the field at one point. A real platypus also uses touch receptors in its bill, and the shrimp's dipole moment is a value chosen for this game. The sensitivity of the bill, about 50 µV/cm, is a measured value (Scheich et al., Nature 319, 1986).

In class
On your own

The hunt

From here on your mother does not help. The water is murky and a shrimp is hiding in the silt. Find it from its field; then you swim round behind its tail, work out how far away it is, and strike. A mistake at any point and it gets away; you go home, your mother explains what went wrong, and you try another shrimp.

In the full lesson a second project follows this one: the young platypus has to dodge lightning in a thunderstorm.
